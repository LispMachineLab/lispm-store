A grid of five squares by five, to be filled.  Every move turns over a square and the four beside
it, filling those that are empty and emptying those that are filled, so a move that fills one
square undoes others.

## Playing

- Click a square to make the move there.
- Or move to it with the arrows or `C-f`, `C-b`, `C-n` and `C-p` and press `SPC` or `RET`.
  `TAB` moves right, and `C-a` and `C-e` go to either end of a row.
- `n` or **New Game** starts again; `r` or **Shuffle** starts from a random grid.
- `s` or **Hint** works out a solution and marks the squares to press with a dot.
  `<` and `>` show the other solutions in turn.

A new game begins with a cross of five filled squares in the middle.  The placards along the
window's bottom count the squares filled and the moves made, and the strip under the title bar
says when the grid is full.

The order of the moves does not matter, and making a move twice is the same as not making it,
so a solution is a set of squares to press once each.  A 5 by 5 grid has four of them; Hint
shows the one with the fewest presses first.  It finds them with Calc, by solving the grid as a
set of equations in arithmetic modulo 2, the work of Vincent Belaïche and Jay P. Belanger.

There are also four crackers, on `C-c C-r`, `C-c C-c`, `C-c C-b` and `C-c C-x`, which hunt for
a solution by trial instead: they breed new attempts from random ones, or from the best so far,
and play each attempt on the board as they go, in place of the game in progress.

## Where it comes from

Dave Pearson wrote 5x5 in 1998.  Its rule is the one of *Lights Out*, the handheld game Tiger
Electronics brought out in 1995, in which a press switches a light and its neighbours and the
aim is to put every light out; here the aim is to fill every square instead.
